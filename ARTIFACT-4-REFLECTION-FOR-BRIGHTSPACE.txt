Artifact 4 Reflection

For my fourth portfolio artifact, I selected the Data Challenge Scenarios activity because it demonstrates my ability to apply machine learning concepts to realistic situations rather than only describe them theoretically. The artifact focuses on missing data, imbalanced datasets, and data and concept drift, which are common challenges when developing and maintaining machine learning systems.

I customized this artifact for a professional audience, including instructors, employers, and individuals interested in data analytics or machine learning. Instead of displaying the entire chatbot conversation, I organized the information into clear sections that describe the objective, process, tools, skills demonstrated, and value of the activity. I also simplified some of the technical explanations so that readers without an advanced machine learning background could understand my decisions and reasoning.

One of the most important lessons I learned was that high model accuracy does not always mean that a model is effective. In the fraud-detection scenario, a model achieved 99.5% accuracy while failing to identify almost all fraudulent transactions. This helped me understand why precision, recall, F1-score, and business impact must be considered when evaluating a model. I also learned that missing data should not be handled automatically because the pattern of missingness may contain important information or introduce bias.

The feedback from the AI coach encouraged me to explain my decisions more specifically. For example, I initially stated that I would estimate missing values using similar homes. After receiving feedback, I clarified that I would consider K-nearest neighbors imputation and explained how I would validate it by comparing estimated values with known values. I also expanded my discussion of fraud-detection thresholds by considering investigation capacity, financial risk, customer experience, and different transaction types.

I revised the artifact by summarizing the strongest parts of each scenario, removing repetitive dialogue, and adding explanations of how each decision supports reliable and responsible machine learning. These revisions made the artifact clearer, more professional, and more relevant to my intended audience. This activity strengthened my understanding that machine learning requires continuous evaluation, thoughtful data preparation, and decisions that balance technical performance with real-world impact.
